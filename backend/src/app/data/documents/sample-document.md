## The Pied Piper of Hamelin

Once upon a time, the beautiful town of Hamelin faced a terrible disaster. It was completely overrun by millions of mice and rats. They were in the kitchens, the bedrooms, the bakeries, and the streets. The townspeople were desperate, so the Mayor announced a massive reward of ten thousand gold coins to anyone who could rid the town of the pests.

The next day, a mysterious stranger arrived. He wore a long, colourful coat and carried a magical pipe. He claimed he was a master rat-catcher and struck a deal with the Mayor to clear the town.

Standing in the town square, the Piper put the pipe to his lips and played a strange, mesmerizing melody.

Instantly, from every corner of Hamelin, the mice and rats poured out of the houses. They gathered in an enormous swarm, completely enchanted by the music. The Piper walked slowly through the gates, continuing his tune, leading the endless trail of rodents all the way to the River Weser. Mesmerized by the sound, the animals jumped straight into the rushing water and drowned.

The town celebrated wildly. However, when the Piper returned to collect his promised gold coins, the greedy Mayor and the ungrateful townspeople changed their minds. "The job was too easy!" the Mayor laughed, refusing to pay the agreed amount and offering just a tiny fraction instead. Angry and betrayed, the Piper warned them they would regret breaking their word.

On the morning of Saint John's Day, the Piper returned. He pulled out his pipe once more, but this time, he played a completely different, sweet melody.

Instead of mice, all the children of the town came running out of their homes, laughing and dancing to the beautiful music. Spellbound, they followed the Piper out of the town gates and toward a large, hollow mountain. A massive door opened up in the rockside; the Piper walked inside, and all the children followed him. The stone door shut tightly behind them, and they vanished forever. Only two children—one who was hard of hearing and another who had hurt her leg—were left behind to tell the grief-stricken townspeople what had happened. The citizens of Hamelin spent the rest of their lives regretting their greed and dishonesty.
